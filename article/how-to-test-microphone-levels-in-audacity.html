How to Test Microphone Levels in Audacity

Testing your microphone signal levels in Audacity prior to tracking ensures your audio is clean, intelligible, and free from digital distortion. By utilizing the built-in Recording Meter, you can monitor incoming audio in real time, adjust your hardware and software gain, and establish healthy headroom before committing a single take to the timeline.

1. Select Your Input Device

Before testing the signal, make sure Audacity is listening to the correct microphone:

  • Locate the Audio Setup button in the top toolbar (or the Device Toolbar in older versions).
  • Under Recording Device, select your external microphone or audio interface.
  • Set the recording channels to 1 (Mono) for a single microphone or 2 (Stereo) if you are using a stereo pair.

2. Enable Live Meter Monitoring

Audacity allows you to view input levels without actually recording:

  • Find the Recording Meter Toolbar, indicated by the microphone icon and a decibel (dB) scale.
  • Click anywhere directly on the meter or click the microphone icon and select Start Monitoring.
  • The meter will activate and display green, yellow, or red bars that respond to incoming sound in real time.

3. Perform a Realistic Sound Check

Speak, sing, or play your instrument into the microphone exactly as you plan to during your performance. Deliver the loudest dynamic level you expect to produce during the session to test worst-case peak levels.

4. Read the Decibel Scale

Observe the peak indicator bars on the meter:

  • Target Range: Your normal performance should hover between -18 dB and -12 dB.
  • Peaks: Your loudest moments should peak around -6 dB.
  • Clipping: If the meter hits 0 dB, the signal is clipping. This produces permanent digital distortion and must be avoided.

5. Adjust the Input Gain

If your levels are consistently too quiet (below -24 dB) or too loud (hitting the red zone above -6 dB), adjust the volume:

  • Hardware First: If you use an audio interface, USB microphone, or mixer with a physical gain knob, turn the gain up or down on the physical unit first.
  • Software Slider: Adjust the Recording Volume Slider in Audacity (the slider next to the microphone icon) to fine-tune the digital input level.

Once your peaks sit consistently between -12 dB and -6 dB, your microphone level is calibrated and ready for tracking.
