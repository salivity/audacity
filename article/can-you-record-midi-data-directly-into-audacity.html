Can You Record MIDI Data Directly into Audacity?

Audacity cannot record MIDI performance data directly from a MIDI keyboard, controller, or electronic instrument. While Audacity is a powerful open-source audio editor, it is primarily designed to manipulate digitized audio waveforms rather than process real-time MIDI event data. This guide explains Audacity’s specific limitations regarding MIDI, what you can do with MIDI files inside the software, and the best workarounds for capturing your instrument's performance.

Audacity’s MIDI Limitations

Audacity lacks a built-in MIDI sequencer and cannot receive incoming MIDI messages (such as Note On, Note Off, velocity, or pitch bend) via USB or standard MIDI interfaces. When you strike a key on a MIDI controller connected to your computer, Audacity cannot log those performance commands onto a track.

To capture raw MIDI data for sequencing or editing, you must use a full-featured Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) such as Cakewalk, Reaper, GarageBand, Ableton Live, or Tracktion Waveform.

What MIDI Features Audacity Does Support

Although Audacity cannot record MIDI input, it does offer rudimentary support for pre-existing MIDI files:

  • Importing and Viewing: You can import standard MIDI files (.mid format) into Audacity by navigating to File > Import > MIDI. The software displays the data as a "Note Track."
  • Basic Playback: Audacity can play back these Note Tracks using your operating system's built-in software synthesizer.
  • Limited Editing: Users can perform basic cut, copy, paste, and transposition adjustments on existing Note Tracks, though the editing interface is basic compared to dedicated piano-roll editors.

Workarounds to Capture Your Performance in Audacity

If you want to use Audacity in your production workflow, you can still record the sound of your performance using one of the following methods:

1. Record the Instrument's Audio Output

If your MIDI keyboard or synthesizer contains its own built-in sound engine and has audio outputs (such as a 1/4-inch line out or a 3.5mm headphone jack), connect those outputs to your computer's audio interface or line-in port. In Audacity, select the interface as your recording device and hit Record. You will capture the audio rendition of your performance, though not the editable MIDI notes.

2. Route Software Instruments via Audio Loopback

If you are using a MIDI controller to play a standalone virtual instrument (VSTi) on your computer, you can record that instrument's sound into Audacity using software loopback:

  • Enable Windows WASAPI as your Audio Host in Audacity.
  • Select your speakers or headphones with the label (loopback) as the recording device.
  • Arm Audacity to record while playing the standalone software instrument.

3. Record MIDI in a Dedicated DAW and Export

The standard workflow for musicians is to record MIDI data directly into a dedicated DAW that features full MIDI sequencing and virtual instrument hosting. Once the MIDI performance is recorded and edited to your liking, render or export the track as a WAV or MP3 file, which can then be imported into Audacity for final mixing, mastering, or cleanup.
