How to Customize Keyboard Shortcuts in Audacity

Customizing keyboard shortcuts in Audacity is one of the most effective ways to streamline your audio editing workflow, allowing you to bypass menus and perform repetitive tasks instantly. This guide covers how to access Audacity’s keyboard settings, search for specific commands, assign custom keybindings without conflicts, and export your shortcut profiles. You will also discover essential editing actions that benefit the most from custom shortcuts to dramatically reduce editing time.

Accessing the Keyboard Preferences

To begin modifying your keybindings, open the configuration settings in Audacity:

  1. Launch Audacity.
  2. Open the Preferences window:
    • Windows/Linux: Navigate to Edit > Preferences (or press Ctrl + P).
    • macOS: Navigate to Audacity > Preferences (or press Cmd + ,).
  3. Select Keyboard from the left-hand navigation sidebar.

How to Assign Custom Shortcuts

Once in the Keyboard preferences panel, follow these steps to bind a key to any command:

  1. Locate the Command: Use the search bar at the top or toggle between View by: Name, Tree, or Key to find the specific function you want to rebind.
  2. Select the Command: Click on the command name in the list to highlight it.
  3. Input the Shortcut: Click inside the text box next to the Set button at the bottom of the window.
  4. Press Your Key Combination: Press the actual keys on your keyboard (e.g., a single key like S or a combination like Ctrl + Shift + X).
  5. Confirm the Binding: Click the Set button.
  6. Save Changes: Click OK at the bottom right to apply your new shortcuts.

Note: If your chosen combination is already assigned to another command, Audacity will display a conflict warning. You must clear or reassign the existing command before binding it to the new one.

High-Impact Shortcuts to Accelerate Editing

Audacity leaves several useful tools unassigned by default, while others require awkward multi-key combinations. Assigning these high-frequency actions to single-key or easy-to-reach combinations can drastically speed up your process:

  • Split Clip: Split audio tracks instantly at the playhead. (Common choice: B or S).
  • Silence Audio: Mute background noises or coughs in a highlighted section. (Default is Ctrl + L; rebinding to a single key speeds up voiceover cleanup).
  • Ripple Delete: Delete a selection and shift subsequent audio back to close the gap.
  • Zoom to Selection: Zoom in immediately on highlighted audio for precise cuts.
  • Repeat Last Effect: Reapply your previous effect (such as Compression, EQ, or Noise Reduction) without opening a menu.

Exporting and Backing Up Shortcut Profiles

Audacity allows you to save custom keyboard layouts so you can transfer them to another computer or restore them after a software update:

  • Export: In the Keyboard preferences menu, click the Export button to save your current configuration as an XML file.
  • Import: Click Import to load a previously saved configuration file.
  • Reset: If you ever need to return to the original bindings, click the Defaults button to restore Audacity's factory settings.
