How to Simulate Small Room Acoustics in Audacity

Simulating small room acoustics in Audacity involves using built-in equalization and reverb effects to transform dry, direct dialogue into sound that feels naturally situated in an intimate, treated space. By controlling room size, early reflections, decay times, and high-frequency absorption, you can prevent your podcast dialogue from sounding sterile while maintaining vocal clarity.

1. Prepare Your Dialogue Track

Start with a clean, dry recording with minimal background noise.

  1. Select your entire dialogue track by pressing Ctrl + A (Windows) or Cmd + A (Mac).
  2. Ensure the track is mono. Most podcast speech is captured and processed in mono to maintain a consistent center image.

2. Apply the Reverb Effect

Audacity's native Reverb plugin allows you to shape acoustic boundaries accurately.

  1. Go to Effect > Delay and Reverb > Reverb.
  2. Adjust the sliders using the following targets for a small, treated podcast room:
    • Room Size (%): Set between 10% and 25%. A low value mimics walls that are close to the microphone.
    • Pre-delay (ms): Set between 5 ms and 15 ms. This creates a slight gap before reflections hit, preserving the intelligibility of the initial consonants.
    • Reverberance (%): Set to 15% – 30%. This keeps the reverberation tail extremely short so it does not muddy continuous speech.
    • Damping (%): Set between 60% and 80%. Higher damping simulates soft materials—such as acoustic foam, carpet, and curtains—which absorb high frequencies quickly.
    • Tone Low (%): Set around 30% – 50% to avoid low-end rumble or an unnatural "boomy" effect.
    • Tone High (%): Set around 40% – 60% to smooth out harsh top-end reflections.
    • Wet Gain (dB): Set between -18 dB and -26 dB. The wet signal must remain subtle; you only want a hint of reflection beneath the primary voice.
    • Dry Gain (dB): Keep at 0 dB to ensure the original recording stays prominent.
    • Stereo Width (%): If working on a stereo track, set this to 0% – 30% to keep reflections centered and intimate.
  3. Click Preview to audition the sound, then click Apply once the balance is natural.

3. Tame Reflections with Equalization

Natural rooms rarely reflect high and low frequencies evenly. Fine-tuning the frequency response ensures the room sound blends seamlessly:

  1. Select the track and open Effect > EQ and Filters > Filter Curve EQ.
  2. Apply a gentle high-shelf cut starting around 5 kHz to 8 kHz, dropping by 1.5 dB to 3 dB. This simulates air absorption and wall dampening.
  3. Check the 250 Hz to 500 Hz range. If the voice begins to sound boxy after applying reverb, create a gentle dip of 1 dB to 2 dB in this zone to clear up the midrange.
  4. Click Apply.

4. Fine-Tune with A/B Testing

To ensure the effect does not distract listeners:

  • Use the Undo (Ctrl + Z / Cmd + Z) and Redo (Ctrl + Y / Cmd + Shift + Z) commands to switch back and forth between the processed and unprocessed audio.
  • Listen on both headphones and desktop speakers. The room acoustic should provide a sense of physical space without being consciously noticeable during normal listening.
