What Is Audacity Spectral Selection Toolbar Used For?

The Spectral Selection toolbar in Audacity is a specialized tool designed to view, select, and edit specific frequency bands within an audio track alongside traditional time selections. This guide covers what the toolbar does, how it interacts with Audacity’s Spectrogram view, its primary use cases in audio restoration, and the core controls it provides for precise sound manipulation.

Understanding the Spectral Selection Toolbar

In standard audio editing, selections are made purely across the horizontal time domain (seconds and minutes). However, the Spectral Selection toolbar introduces vertical frequency-based control. When working in the Spectrogram track view, this toolbar enables users to define both the time boundaries and the exact frequency boundaries (measured in Hertz) of an audio signal.

Instead of applying an effect to the entire frequency spectrum of a time selection, the toolbar allows edits to target only a specific frequency band—such as removing a whistle while leaving a speaker's voice untouched.

Primary Uses in Audacity

  • Targeted Noise Removal: Traditional equalizers and noise-reduction tools often affect broad frequency bands, which can degrade audio quality. With spectral selection, editors can pinpoint isolated, unwanted sounds—such as microphone hums, phone beeps, finger squeaks on guitar strings, or audience coughs—and eliminate them using spectral editing tools.
  • Surgical Audio Restoration: The toolbar is essential when using Audacity’s built-in spectral effects, such as Spectral Edit Notch, Spectral Edit Multi Tool, and Spectral Edit Parametric EQ. These effects rely directly on the frequency limits set in the Spectral Selection toolbar to filter out or boost only the targeted frequencies.
  • Precise Frequency Measurements: Mouse dragging in a spectrogram can be imprecise. The toolbar allows users to type exact numeric frequency values to target known frequencies precisely (for instance, a 60 Hz or 50 Hz electrical ground hum).
  • Audio Analysis: Sound designers and engineers use the toolbar to examine the exact acoustic characteristics of specific sounds, measuring both the center frequency and the bandwidth of resonances or harmonics.

Key Controls on the Toolbar

The toolbar offers two main display modes, toggled via a drop-down menu or right-click:

  1. Center Frequency and Width: Displays the midpoint of the selected frequency range and how wide the selection extends above and below that center.
  2. Low and High Frequency: Displays the explicit bottom and top frequencies (in Hz) bounding the current selection.

Each field is interactive, allowing users to enter custom numerical values to reposition or resize the vertical selection box on the spectrogram.

How to Enable and Use It

  1. Enable the toolbar by navigating to View > Toolbars > Spectral Selection Toolbar.
  2. Change the track view to reveal frequency data by clicking the track name dropdown and selecting Spectrogram (or Multi-view to see both the waveform and spectrogram simultaneously).
  3. Click and drag within the spectrogram to create a box that spans both a duration of time and a range of frequencies.
  4. Fine-tune the boundaries using the numeric inputs on the Spectral Selection toolbar before applying spectral restoration effects.
