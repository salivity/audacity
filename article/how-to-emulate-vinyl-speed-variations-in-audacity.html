How to Emulate Vinyl Speed Variations in Audacity

Audacity's Change Speed effect accurately emulates vinyl turntable speed variations by simultaneously altering both the pitch and tempo of an audio track, directly mimicking the physical mechanics of a spinning record. While modern digital audio workstations often separate pitch from playback speed using complex time-stretching algorithms, the Change Speed tool couples them together. This article explains the underlying digital processing, the built-in vinyl RPM conversion presets, and how to apply these mechanics to recreate classic vinyl playback artifacts.

The Relationship Between Vinyl Mechanics and Digital Resampling

When a physical vinyl record spins faster or slower on a turntable, the stylus traverses the microscopic grooves at a varying rate. Increasing the motor speed causes the stylus to read more waveforms per second, which raises the frequency (pitch) and reduces the total playback duration (tempo). Conversely, slowing the turntable down stretches the waveforms out, dropping the pitch and dragging out the duration.

Audacity’s Change Speed effect replicates this physical phenomenon through mathematical resampling rather than algorithmic phase vocoding. Instead of attempting to keep the pitch steady while altering length (like the "Change Tempo" effect) or keeping length steady while altering key (like the "Change Pitch" effect), Change Speed recalculates the relationship between the audio samples and the project sample rate. This mimics an analog motor speeding up or slowing down.

Built-In Vinyl RPM Presets

Audacity includes dedicated vinyl presets within the Change Speed interface to instantly replicate standard turntable RPM (revolutions per minute) switches. The interface provides standard conversions between:

  • 33⅓ RPM (standard LP speed)
  • 45 RPM (standard singles/EP speed)
  • 78 RPM (vintage shellac record speed)

By selecting a source speed and a target speed, Audacity applies the exact mathematical ratio of the physical speed change:

  • 33⅓ RPM to 45 RPM: Multiplies the speed by 1.35, resulting in a 35% speed increase and a corresponding pitch shift upward of approximately 5.18 semitones. This accurately recreates the classic "chipmunk" effect heard when playing an LP at single speed.
  • 45 RPM to 33⅓ RPM: Multiplies the speed by roughly 0.74, lowering both tempo and pitch by about 26%, recreating the deep, dragged-out sound of a 45 played on an LP setting.

Emulating Dynamic Speed Fluctuations

Mechanical turntables do not just switch between fixed speeds; they also suffer from dynamic imperfections like motor drag, startup/stop friction, and subtle speed drift (wow and flutter).

To emulate these dynamic variations rather than a flat, static speed switch, Audacity’s Time Track feature can be combined with the Change Speed methodology:

  1. Turntable Spin-Down / Tape Stop: Applying a decelerating speed envelope drops the pitch continuously to near-zero while simultaneously slowing the audio to a halt, capturing the sound of cutting power to a turntable motor while the needle is down.
  2. Wow and Flutter: Minor, oscillating adjustments to speed (deviations of 0.1% to 1%) replicate belt-drive instability, warped vinyl discs, or off-center spindle holes, giving modern digital recordings an authentic vintage pitch waver.
