How to Use Sound Activated Recording in Audacity

Sound Activated Recording in Audacity allows users to automatically start and pause recordings based on the volume of incoming audio, eliminating long stretches of dead air. This guide explains what the feature does, how to enable it, and the exact steps required to set its threshold level for clean, efficient audio capture.

What is Sound Activated Recording?

Sound Activated Recording is an automated recording mode within Audacity. Instead of recording continuously from the moment you press the Record button, Audacity monitors the input volume. The software begins recording data only when the input sound exceeds a defined decibel (dB) level. As soon as the sound drops below that level, Audacity automatically pauses, resuming only when the sound volume rises again.

This feature is ideal for:

  • Voice dictation and note-taking.
  • Recording long interviews with natural pauses.
  • Monitoring wildlife, room acoustics, or surveillance audio.
  • Saving disk space and reducing editing time by eliminating silent passages.

How to Enable Sound Activated Recording

  1. Open Audacity.
  2. Click on the Transport menu at the top of the screen.
  3. Hover over Transport Options.
  4. Click on Sound Activated Recording (on/off) to check and enable it.

Once checked, standard recording will not capture silence; it will pause and wait for incoming audio that meets the volume threshold.

How to Set the Activation Threshold

The activation threshold determines how loud an input must be to trigger the recording.

  1. Click on the Transport menu.
  2. Hover over Transport Options.
  3. Select Sound Activation Level....
  4. In the dialog box that appears, adjust the slider or type a value in decibels (dB).
  5. Click OK to save your settings.

Choosing the Correct Decibel (dB) Level

Decibels in digital audio are measured in negative values, where 0 dB is the absolute maximum (loudest) level and lower numbers (such as -60 dB) represent very quiet sounds.

  • High sensitivity (-50 dB to -40 dB): Triggers on very quiet sounds, such as whispers or low room noise. Best used in sound-treated studios.
  • Moderate sensitivity (-30 dB to -20 dB): The standard range for speech, singing, or standard instruments. This range captures normal voice levels while ignoring typical background hiss or computer fans.
  • Low sensitivity (-15 dB to -6 dB): Only captures very loud sounds, ignoring normal speech and moderate ambient noise.

Testing and Optimizing Your Settings

To find the ideal threshold:

  1. Observe the Audacity recording meter while remaining silent to find your room's baseline ambient noise (for example, -45 dB).
  2. Speak or play your instrument at your normal performance volume to see your active level (for example, -12 dB).
  3. Set your threshold midway between the ambient noise and your performance level (for example, -30 dB).
  4. Press the Record button (or press R). The recording head will remain stationary until you make a sound above the configured threshold, at which point it will immediately begin capturing audio.
