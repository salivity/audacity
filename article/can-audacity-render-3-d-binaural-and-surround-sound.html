Can Audacity Render 3D Binaural and Surround Sound?

Audacity can render 3D binaural audio and basic multi-channel surround sound, but it cannot do so natively. Because it lacks integrated spatial panning tools and modern object-based audio engines, users must rely on third-party VST plugins for binaural processing or use Audacity's advanced multi-channel mixing settings for surround sound. This guide breaks down what Audacity can and cannot achieve with spatial audio formats and outlines how to produce these mixes.

Native Capabilities and Limitations

Audacity does not feature a built-in 3D spatial panner, an Ambisonics decoders, or native support for modern immersive formats like Dolby Atmos. By default, its mixing environment operates strictly in standard stereo and mono panning. However, Audacity does support multi-channel audio tracks, which allows it to import, edit, and export up to 32 separate audio channels simultaneously.

Rendering Surround Sound (5.1 and 7.1)

Audacity can export traditional channel-based surround sound, such as 5.1 or 7.1 setups. However, you cannot dynamically pan a sound across a virtual room using visual positioning tools.

To render surround sound in Audacity:

  1. Enable Multi-Channel Export: Navigate to Edit > Preferences > Import / Export and select Use advanced mixing options.
  2. Assign Channels: Create discrete mono tracks for each speaker channel (e.g., Left, Right, Center, LFE/Subwoofer, Left Surround, Right Surround).
  3. Export: Go to File > Export Audio. The Advanced Mixing Options dialog will appear, allowing you to map each project track to an individual channel output in formats that support multi-channel audio, such as WAV, AIFF, or FLAC.

Rendering 3D Binaural Audio

Binaural audio uses Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTF) to simulate human hearing, delivering a 3D soundstage through standard stereo headphones. While Audacity does not generate HRTF profiles natively, it fully supports third-party VST3, VST2, and LADSPA plugins that can.

To render 3D binaural sound in Audacity:

  1. Install a Binaural Spatializer Plugin: Download and install free or commercial spatializer plugins, such as DearVR MICRO or the IEM Plugin Suite.
  2. Enable the Plugin: Open Audacity, go to Effect > Plugin Manager, locate the plugin, and click Enable.
  3. Apply Spatial Positioning: Apply the plugin to a track. Use the plugin interface to adjust the sound source's azimuth (horizontal angle), elevation (vertical position), and distance.
  4. Export as Stereo: Since binaural spatialization encodes spatial cues into a standard two-channel signal, simply export your final mix as a standard stereo WAV or MP3 file.

Limitations with Modern Spatial Formats

If your goal is to deliver commercial spatial audio formats, Audacity is not the right tool:

  • Dolby Atmos / Apple Spatial Audio: Audacity cannot embed the spatial metadata or render the multi-bed configurations required by Dolby Atmos ADM BWF formats.
  • Higher-Order Ambisonics (HOA): While Audacity can technically store B-format multi-channel audio, it cannot monitor or decode Ambisonics in real time.

Summary

Audacity can render 3D binaural audio using third-party HRTF plugins and can export traditional 5.1/7.1 surround sound using its advanced mixing setup. However, for full-featured surround panning, real-time 3D monitoring, or metadata-driven formats like Dolby Atmos, a dedicated digital audio workstation (DAW) such as Reaper, Logic Pro, or Pro Tools is required.
