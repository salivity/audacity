How to Color-Code Clips in Audacity

Audacity does not currently support direct color-coding for individual audio clips on the same track, but it does offer track-level color customization and alternative organizational tools to manage complex sessions. While you cannot assign distinct colors to individual cut segments on a single timeline, you can visually organize your project by changing waveform colors across separate tracks, utilizing label tracks, and renaming clips.

The Direct Answer: Individual Clips vs. Tracks

You cannot right-click an individual audio clip on a track and assign it a unique color. Audacity applies color styling uniformly across an entire track's waveform. If you need distinct colors for specific parts of an interview, sound effects, or musical takes, you must move those clips onto separate tracks that use different color schemes.

How to Change Track Waveform Colors

Audacity provides four distinct color styles for track waveforms. To apply different colors to separate tracks:

  1. Locate the Track Control Panel on the far left side of the track you want to customize.
  2. Click the track title or the downward-facing arrow to open the track menu.
  3. Hover over the Waveform Color option.
  4. Select one of the four available presets: Instrument 1, Instrument 2, Instrument 3, or Instrument 4.

Each option changes the hue of all waveforms on that specific track, allowing you to establish a functional multi-track color hierarchy.

Alternative Organizational Methods

Since clip-level coloring is restricted, alternative workflows provide similar clarity:

  • Use Label Tracks: Create a dedicated label track by going to Tracks > Add New > Label Track. Highlight a clip or time range and press Ctrl + B (or Cmd + B on macOS) to create a label. Labels anchor directly beneath your clips and provide clear text-based landmarks across your timeline.
  • Rename Audio Clips: Double-click the header bar of any individual clip (or right-click the clip header and select Rename Clip) to assign unique names to takes, segments, or sound elements.
  • Mute and Solo Organization: Placing different types of clips on distinct, color-differentiated tracks makes it significantly easier to solo or mute entire categories of sound during editing and mixing.
