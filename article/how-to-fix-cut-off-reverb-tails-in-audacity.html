How to Fix Cut-Off Reverb Tails in Audacity

When applying reverb to an audio clip in Audacity, the decay is often cut off abruptly because the software only processes the exact selection you have highlighted. To prevent this truncation, you must provide extra space at the end of the clip so the reverberation has room to fade out naturally. This guide explains how to extend your audio track using generated silence or track boundaries to preserve full, smooth reverb tails.

The Standard Fix: Generating Silence

The most reliable method to prevent truncated reverb tails is appending silent audio to your clip before applying the effect.

  1. Place the cursor at the end of your clip: Use the Selection Tool to click precisely at the end of the audio waveform.
  2. Generate silence: Navigate to the top menu and select Generate > Silence.
  3. Set the duration: Choose a duration that matches or exceeds your intended decay time. For standard reverb settings, 2 to 5 seconds is usually sufficient. Click Generate.
  4. Select both the audio and the silence: Click and drag to highlight the original audio clip along with the newly generated silence.
  5. Apply the reverb: Go to Effect > Delay and Reverb > Reverb, adjust your settings, and click Apply. The reverb will now ring out into the silent region and decay naturally to absolute zero without cutting off.

Using the Clip Boundary Method (Audacity 3.2 and Newer)

If you are using real-time effects added via the track control panel, Audacity handles playback past clip edges differently.

  1. Extend the clip border: Add an empty audio clip or silence right after your track audio to prevent the playback engine from stopping immediately when the main waveform ends.
  2. Render the track: Once your real-time reverb is configured, go to Tracks > Mix > Mix and Render. Ensure that your selection extends a few seconds past the last note so the resulting rendered track captures the full decay tail without truncation.
