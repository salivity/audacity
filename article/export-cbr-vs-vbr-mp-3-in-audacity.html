Export CBR vs VBR MP3 in Audacity

Exporting an MP3 in Audacity requires choosing between Constant Bitrate (CBR) and Variable Bitrate (VBR), two encoding methods that directly impact audio fidelity and final file size. This guide explains the core differences between CBR and VBR and walks you through the exact steps to configure and export your audio using either format in Audacity.

Understanding CBR vs. VBR

  • Constant Bitrate (CBR): Encodes the entire audio track using a fixed data rate (such as 128 kbps or 320 kbps) from beginning to end. It provides guaranteed compatibility with older media players, predictable file sizes, and seamless playback for looping audio.
  • Variable Bitrate (VBR): Dynamically adjusts the data rate based on the complexity of the sound. Simple segments, such as silence or quiet speech, use less data, while complex segments, like layered music, receive higher bitrates. This produces higher overall sound quality at a smaller average file size compared to CBR.

Step-by-Step Export Instructions

1. Open the Export Menu

With your audio project open and edited in Audacity:

  1. Click File in the top menu bar.
  2. Navigate to Export Audio... (or Export > Export as MP3 in older versions).

2. Select MP3 Format

In the export dialog window:

  1. Set the Format dropdown to MP3 Files.
  2. Choose your destination folder and name your file.

3. Configure Bitrate Options

Locate the Format Options section at the bottom of the export window to select your bitrate method:

  • To Export with Constant Bitrate (CBR):
    1. Set Bitrate Mode to Constant.
    2. Under the Quality dropdown, select your preferred bitrate (e.g., 320 kbps for maximum quality, 192 kbps for standard music, or 128 kbps for spoken-word podcasts).
  • To Export with Variable Bitrate (VBR):
    1. Set Bitrate Mode to Variable.
    2. Under the Quality dropdown, choose your desired quality level (options range from 0, 220–260 kbps (Extreme/Insane) for highest quality down to lower ranges for smaller files).
    3. Leave the Variable Speed setting on Fast unless you specifically need slower, deeper analysis.

4. Finalize the Export

  1. Choose your channel mode (Stereo, Joint Stereo, or Mono).
  2. Click Export (or Save).
  3. If prompted, edit the metadata tags (Artist Name, Track Title, etc.) and click OK to render the MP3 file.
