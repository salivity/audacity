How to Group Tracks in Audacity for Editing

Synchronizing multiple tracks in Audacity is essential for maintaining time alignment when editing podcasts, multi-mic recordings, or music projects. Audacity accomplishes this through a feature called Sync-Lock Tracks, which links tracks together so that any editing operation that alters the timing or duration of one track automatically applies to the others. This guide explains how to enable Sync-Lock Tracks, how grouping works, and how to perform synchronized edits without losing track alignment.

Enabling Sync-Lock Tracks

To activate synchronized editing across your tracks:

  1. Open your project containing multiple tracks in Audacity.
  2. Click the Tracks menu at the top of the screen.
  3. Select Sync-Lock Tracks from the dropdown menu to enable it (a checkmark will appear next to the option).

Once enabled, a small clock-like icon (the Sync-Lock icon) will appear in the lower-right corner of the Track Control Panel for each linked track. In addition, when you select a region on one track, a patterned background will appear in the corresponding time range across the linked tracks to indicate that they are synchronized.

How Audacity Defines Track Groups

Audacity automatically groups tracks based on their vertical arrangement in the project window:

  • Contiguous Audio Tracks: Consecutive audio tracks placed directly above or below each other form a single Sync-Locked group.
  • Separating Groups with Label Tracks: A Label Track acts as a boundary. If you have multiple audio tracks above a Label Track and more audio tracks below it, Audacity treats them as two distinct synchronized groups. The Label Track itself synchronizes with the audio tracks directly above it.
  • Rearranging Groups: You can drag tracks up or down by clicking and dragging their Track Control Panel to change which tracks are grouped together.

Performing Synchronized Edits

With Sync-Lock active, editing actions that modify track length will maintain the relative timing between grouped tracks:

  • Deleting or Cutting: Selecting and deleting a section of audio from one track will delete the identical time range across all synchronized tracks, preventing desynchronization.
  • Inserting Audio or Silence: Pasting audio or inserting silence into one track pushes the audio forward across all linked tracks by the same duration.
  • Time Shifting: Moving clips with the Time Shift tool (or selecting and moving clips in newer Audacity versions) will move the corresponding time region on all grouped tracks.

Disabling Sync-Lock Tracks

When you need to make timing adjustments to an individual track without affecting the rest of the project, disable the feature by opening the Tracks menu and clicking Sync-Lock Tracks again to uncheck it.
