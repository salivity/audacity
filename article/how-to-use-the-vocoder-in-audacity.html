How to Use the Vocoder in Audacity

The Vocoder effect in Audacity allows you to blend two separate audio sources to create classic robotic speech, synthesized vocal harmonies, and unique sound design textures. This article explains what the Vocoder effect does, defines the specific roles of the carrier and modulator signals, and provides a direct, step-by-step walkthrough on how to properly route both channels in Audacity to generate the effect.

What Is the Vocoder Effect?

A vocoder (voice encoder) works by analyzing the frequency characteristics of one audio signal and applying them to the harmonic structure of another.

The effect requires two inputs:

  • The Modulator: This is typically a dynamic, articulate sound such as a spoken or sung vocal track. It acts as the filter, dictating the phrasing, rhythm, and consonant shapes.
  • The Carrier: This is a harmonically rich sound with continuous frequency content, such as a synthesizer chord, a saw-wave tone, or white noise. It provides the pitch, tone, and musical timbre.

When processed, the vocoder analyzes the spectral envelope of the modulator through a bank of band-pass filters and uses those levels to shape the carrier. The result is an instrument that appears to "speak" or "sing."

How to Route Carrier and Modulator Signals in Audacity

Audacity's built-in Vocoder effect does not use sidechain routing like standard digital audio workstations (DAWs). Instead, it processes a single stereo track, where one channel serves as the carrier and the other serves as the modulator. By default, Audacity expects the Left channel to be the Carrier and the Right channel to be the Modulator.

Follow these steps to route and combine your tracks:

Step 1: Prepare Two Mono Tracks

  1. Import or record your carrier signal (synthesizer, sustained pad, or noise) on one track.
  2. Import or record your modulator signal (vocal speech or singing) on a second track.
  3. Ensure both tracks are set to Mono. If a track is in stereo, go to Tracks > Mix > Mix Stereo Down to Mono.

Step 2: Arrange the Track Order

  1. Place the Carrier track directly above the Modulator track in the timeline.
  2. Align the timing of both tracks so the carrier plays continuously across the duration of the vocal track.

Step 3: Combine Tracks into a Single Stereo Track

  1. Click the downward-pointing arrow next to the track name on the top track (the Carrier) to open its track control menu.
  2. Select Make Stereo Track.
  3. Audacity will merge the two mono tracks into a single stereo track. The top track (Carrier) automatically becomes the Left Channel, and the bottom track (Modulator) becomes the Right Channel.

Applying the Vocoder Effect

Once your stereo track is created, apply the effect:

  1. Select the entire combined stereo track by clicking in the track control panel on the left.
  2. Go to the top menu and select Effect > Vocoder (in newer Audacity versions, it may be categorized under Effect > Special > Vocoder).
  3. In the Vocoder dialogue box, configure your settings:
    • Distance: Adjusts the output stereo widening.
    • Number of bands: Sets how many filter bands are used. Higher values (e.g., 40 to 100) yield more intelligible speech, while lower values (e.g., 8 to 16) produce a more vintage, robotic sound.
    • Carrier choice: Confirm this matches your routing (default is "Left").
  4. Click Preview to audition the sound, then click Apply to render the vocoded audio.

The resulting audio will output as a processed stereo track featuring the synthesizer's pitch articulated by the vocal track's words.
