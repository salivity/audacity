Paste Audio in Audacity Without Overwriting

Pasting new audio into an existing project often risks overwriting current content or throwing downstream clips out of sync. In Audacity, you can cleanly insert new audio into an existing track and automatically push all downstream audio to the right. This guide outlines the exact settings and methods required to perform an insert paste without replacing or damaging downstream content.

Step 1: Enable Clip Movement in Preferences

To ensure that pasting audio automatically pushes existing downstream audio forward along the timeline, Audacity must be configured to move downstream clips:

  1. Open Edit from the top menu (or Audacity on macOS) and select Preferences.
  2. Click on the Track Behaviors tab in the left sidebar.
  3. Check the box labeled Editing a clip can move other clips.
  4. Click OK to save your settings.

Step 2: Position the Cursor Without Selecting a Region

Overwriting usually occurs when a section of the track is highlighted prior to pasting.

  1. Copy your desired audio to the clipboard using Ctrl + C (Windows) or Cmd + C (Mac).
  2. Go to the target track.
  3. Click once on the exact point where you want the new audio to start. Ensure you do not click and drag, as highlighting any duration will cause the pasted audio to replace the highlighted selection.

Step 3: Insert the Audio

  1. Press Ctrl + V (Windows) or Cmd + V (Mac) to paste.
  2. The new audio will be inserted at the cursor position.
  3. The original audio to the right of the cursor will automatically split and shift downstream by the exact duration of the inserted clip.

Alternative Method: Split and Drag

If you prefer visual control before pasting:

  1. Place the cursor at the desired insertion point.
  2. Press Ctrl + I (Windows) or Cmd + I (Mac) to split the clip at the playhead.
  3. Click and hold the clip header of the downstream section and drag it to the right to create an empty gap.
  4. Place the cursor inside the gap or at the split point and press Ctrl + V (Windows) or Cmd + V (Mac).
