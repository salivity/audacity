How to Remove Echo from Audio in Audacity

Recording an interview in a room with bare walls often introduces unwanted room echo and hollow resonance that can distract listeners. While completely eliminating room reverb from a single audio track is one of the toughest audio engineering challenges, Audacity provides several powerful built-in tools to significantly minimize these reflections. By applying strategic equalization, targeted noise gating, and subtle compression, you can tame the resonance, eliminate trailing room tails, and restore clarity to your dialogue.

Step 1: Use an Equalizer to Cut Muddy Resonance

Room echo typically builds up in the low-mid frequencies between 200 Hz and 500 Hz, making speech sound boomy or muffled.

  1. Select your entire track by pressing Ctrl + A (Windows) or Cmd + A (Mac).
  2. Go to Effect > Filter Curve EQ or Graphic EQ.
  3. Apply a high-pass filter by cutting frequencies below 80 Hz to eliminate low-end rumble.
  4. Locate the 200 Hz to 400 Hz range and gently lower it by 2 to 4 dB. This cleans up the "boxy" acoustic reflections without stripping the natural warmth from the speaker's voice.
  5. Apply a slight boost around 2 kHz to 5 kHz to increase vocal presence and intelligibility, then click Apply.

Step 2: Apply a Noise Gate to Cut Echo Tails

Echo becomes most noticeable in the natural pauses between words and sentences. A Noise Gate mutes or drastically reduces audio below a certain volume threshold, cutting off the lingering reflections.

  1. Select the entire audio track.
  2. Go to Effect > Noise Removal and Repair > Noise Gate.
  3. Set the Gate threshold to sit just below the speaker’s quietest spoken syllables (typically between -30 dB and -40 dB).
  4. Set the Level reduction to around -10 dB to -15 dB rather than full silence (-100 dB), as completely cutting the room sound sounds unnatural.
  5. Set the Attack fast (around 10–20 ms) and the Decay/Hold to roughly 100–150 ms to ensure the ends of words are not clipped off abruptly.
  6. Preview the audio and click Apply once the pauses sound clean.

Step 3: Apply Subtle Noise Reduction

If the echo is combined with ambient room hiss, Audacity’s Noise Reduction tool can help tighten the background space.

  1. Highlight a section of the track that contains only room noise (no speaking) for at least a few seconds.
  2. Go to Effect > Noise Removal and Repair > Noise Reduction and click Get Noise Profile.
  3. Select the entire recording, return to the Noise Reduction menu, and set:
    • Noise reduction (dB): 6 to 8 dB
    • Sensitivity: 6
    • Frequency smoothing (bands): 3
  4. Click OK. Avoid aggressive settings, as high noise reduction will make the voice sound metallic and robotic.

Step 4: Level the Voice with Compression

Once the unwanted resonance and tails are diminished, a compressor will balance the volume between loud and soft words to ensure a consistent dialogue level.

  1. Select the entire track.
  2. Navigate to Effect > Volume and Compression > Compressor.
  3. Set the Threshold to around -15 dB to -18 dB, the Ratio between 2:1 and 3:1, and keep the attack time fast.
  4. Click Apply.

If the room echo remains distracting after these native adjustments, consider installing a dedicated third-party de-verb VST plugin, which can be loaded directly into Audacity's effect menu for specialized digital reflection removal.
