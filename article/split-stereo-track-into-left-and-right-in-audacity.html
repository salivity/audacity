Split Stereo Track into Left and Right in Audacity

Splitting a stereo audio track into separate left and right channels in Audacity allows you to edit, process, or mix each channel independently. This guide walks you through the quick, step-by-step process of using the track control panel to separate your stereo audio into distinct tracks, while also explaining the difference between splitting to standard stereo tracks versus mono tracks.

Step 1: Open Your Audio File

Launch Audacity and open your stereo audio file by navigating to File > Open, or simply drag and drop the audio file directly into the Audacity window. Ensure that the track contains two waveforms within a single track panel, indicating it is a stereo file.

Step 2: Open the Track Dropdown Menu

Locate the track control panel on the far left side of the track (where the mute/solo buttons and volume sliders are located).

  1. Click on the track name at the top of this panel (or click the small downward-pointing arrow next to the track name in older versions of Audacity).
  2. A context menu will appear with various track management options.

Step 3: Choose Your Splitting Method

From the dropdown menu, you will see two options for splitting the audio:

  • Split Stereo Track: This separates the audio into two individual tracks. The top track will be assigned entirely to the Left channel (panned 100% Left), and the bottom track will be assigned entirely to the Right channel (panned 100% Right). Use this option if you want to maintain the original stereo balance while editing each side separately.
  • Split Stereo to Mono: This separates the audio into two independent mono tracks. Both the left and right components will be centered in the stereo field (panning slider set to center). Use this option if you want to isolate one channel (such as an interview where two microphones were recorded to separate channels) and have it play evenly across both speakers.

Step 4: Edit the Tracks Independently

Once selected, your single stereo track will immediately divide into two separate horizontal tracks. You can now apply effects, cut, delete, adjust volume, or change the panning sliders for either channel without affecting the other.
