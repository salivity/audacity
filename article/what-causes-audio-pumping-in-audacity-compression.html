What Causes Audio Pumping in Audacity Compression?

Audio pumping and breathing artifacts occur when a dynamic range compressor aggressively attenuates loud vocal peaks and rapidly restores volume during quieter moments, causing background noise and room tone to audibly rush up between words. This article outlines the primary causes of these distracting volume fluctuations when compressing voice recordings in Audacity and explains how to configure your settings to maintain a smooth, natural vocal performance.

Fast Release Times

The release time determines how long the compressor takes to return to normal volume levels after the signal falls below the threshold. If the release time in Audacity is set too short, the compressor recovers almost instantly when a speaker pauses or finishes a sentence. This sudden jump in gain rapidly amplifies the background room tone, air conditioning hum, or the speaker's natural inhalations, creating the distinctive "breathing" sensation. Increasing the release time creates a gradual, transparent transition that hides the volume recovery.

Overly Aggressive Threshold and Ratio

A threshold set too low forces the compressor to act on nearly every part of the vocal performance, rather than just the loudest peaks. When paired with a high compression ratio (such as 6:1 or higher for standard speech), the compressor drastically squashes the natural dynamics of the voice. This constant, heavy-handed attenuation creates extreme volume swings between active speech and pauses, resulting in an unnatural "pumping" effect. For spoken word, moderate ratios between 2:1 and 4:1 paired with a threshold that only catches the louder peaks produce a much more stable output.

Inappropriate Attack Time Settings

The attack time dictates how quickly the compressor reacts once audio crosses the threshold. If the attack is set too fast, it immediately clamps down on initial consonants and transient sounds, causing the volume to dip unnaturally fast at the start of every syllable. Conversely, an attack that is too slow allows initial bursts of sound to pass untouched before abruptly dragging the volume down mid-word, resulting in a wobbly, unstable volume contour.

Automatic Make-Up Gain on Noisy Audio

Audacity includes an option labeled "Make-up gain for 0 dB after compressing." While convenient, this setting automatically boosts the overall level of the processed audio to maximize loudness. If the original recording contains ambient noise or room reflections, heavy compression flattens the voice, and the make-up gain boosts both the voice and the low-level noise floor together. As speech stops and starts, the amplified ambient noise surges in volume. Applying subtle noise reduction or using a noise gate prior to compression prevents room tone from being magnified.
