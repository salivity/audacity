How to Reorder Tracks in Audacity

Audacity allows you to easily reorganize your workspace by dragging track headers vertically to reorder tracks. This guide explains how to use the drag-and-drop feature on the Track Control Panel to reposition your audio streams, along with alternative menu-based methods to move tracks up or down within your project timeline.

How to Drag Tracks Vertically

You can quickly change the vertical order of tracks using your mouse:

  1. Locate the Track Control Panel: This is the box located on the far-left side of every track, containing the track name, Mute and Solo buttons, and volume sliders.
  2. Click and Hold an Empty Area: Click and hold the left mouse button inside an empty space within the control panel. Avoid clicking directly on buttons, sliders, meters, or the track title drop-down.
  3. Drag Vertically: While holding the mouse button, drag the track upward or downward. A horizontal line will appear across the timeline to indicate the new position where the track will be placed.
  4. Release to Place: Release the mouse button once the indicator line is in your desired location. The track will snap into its new position.

Alternative Method: Using the Track Menu

If you prefer using menus or keyboard navigation rather than dragging, you can use the built-in track movement commands:

  1. Click the downward arrow next to the track name (or right-click the track header) to open the Track Menu.
  2. Select one of the placement options:
    • Move Track Up: Moves the track up by one position.
    • Move Track Down: Moves the track down by one position.
    • Move Track to Top: Instantly moves the track to the very top of the project window.
    • Move Track to Bottom: Instantly moves the track to the very bottom of the project window.

Tips for Managing Large Projects

  • Collapse Tracks Before Moving: If you have many tracks, click the upward-pointing arrow at the bottom-left corner of the Track Control Panel to collapse the track height. Collapsed tracks take up less vertical space, making it much easier to drag them across large distances.
  • Keep Stereo Pairs Intact: When working with stereo tracks, dragging the header moves both the left and right channels together automatically.
