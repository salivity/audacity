How to Hot-Plug a USB Microphone in Audacity

USB microphones can be connected and recognized without restarting Audacity by utilizing the software's built-in audio device rescanning feature. While Audacity does not always refresh its input list automatically the moment a device is inserted, users do not need to save, close, and reopen the application. A simple command forces Audacity to detect newly connected hardware immediately without interrupting an ongoing session.

Using the Rescan Audio Devices Feature

To recognize a newly plugged-in USB microphone while keeping Audacity open:

  1. Connect the USB microphone to your computer's USB port and allow the operating system a few seconds to initialize the hardware.
  2. In Audacity, click on the Transport menu in the top navigation bar.
  3. Select Rescan Audio Devices (or press the keyboard shortcut Shift + F5).

Audacity will momentarily poll the system for all connected input and output devices.

Selecting the New Microphone

Once the rescan is complete, the newly attached microphone becomes selectable within the interface:

  1. Click the Audio Setup button located on the top toolbar.
  2. Navigate to Recording Device.
  3. Select the name of your USB microphone from the list.

If using an older layout with the classic Device Toolbar enabled, click the recording input dropdown menu (next to the microphone icon) and choose the new device directly.

Common Troubleshooting Steps

If the microphone still does not appear after running the rescan:

  • Check System Settings: Verify that your operating system (Windows, macOS, or Linux) recognizes the microphone in its primary sound settings. Audacity relies on system-level drivers to detect audio hardware.
  • Verify App Permissions: Modern operating systems require explicit microphone access permissions. Ensure that Audacity is allowed to access your microphone under system privacy settings.
  • Bypass USB Hubs: Connect the microphone directly to the computer rather than through an unpowered USB hub, which can cause connection drops or delayed recognition.
