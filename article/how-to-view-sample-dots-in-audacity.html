How to View Sample Dots in Audacity

Audacity allows users to zoom in close enough to see and manipulate individual audio sample dots along the waveform. This extreme magnification reaches the single-sample level, which is useful for identifying and repairing minor audio defects like clicks, pops, or clipping. This guide explains how to achieve maximum zoom in Audacity, what sample dots represent, and how to edit them directly.

Reaching the Sample Level

You can reach the sample level using any of Audacity's zooming methods:

  • Keyboard Shortcuts: Press Ctrl + 1 (or Cmd + 1 on macOS) repeatedly to zoom in horizontally centered on the cursor or selection.
  • Mouse Wheel: Hold Ctrl (or Cmd) and scroll up with your mouse wheel over the track.
  • Zoom Tool: Click the Zoom Tool (magnifying glass) in the toolbar and left-click repeatedly on the waveform.

Continue zooming in until the solid waveform disappears. Once you cross a specific threshold, Audacity will display a continuous line with distinct circular dots on it.

Understanding Audio Sample Dots

Each visible dot represents a single discrete digital sample recorded at your project's sample rate. For instance, if an audio file was recorded at 44.1 kHz, there are 44,100 individual dots representing every single second of sound.

The vertical position of each dot indicates its amplitude (loudness) at that exact fraction of a millisecond. Seeing audio at this resolution allows you to spot isolated erroneous samples that cause audible clicks or harsh spikes.

Editing Individual Samples

Audacity does not just let you view these samples; it also lets you modify them directly:

  1. Locate the Draw Tool, represented by a pencil icon in the Tools Toolbar.
  2. Click and drag any individual dot up or down to change its amplitude value.
  3. Smooth out sharp, isolated spikes to eliminate clicks or pops without affecting the surrounding audio.

To quickly restore normal viewing after editing, press Ctrl + 3 (or Cmd + 3) to zoom out, or Ctrl + F (or Cmd + F) to fit the entire project back onto the screen.
