How to Fade In and Fade Out Audio in Audacity

Applying a gradual fade in or fade out in Audacity allows you to create seamless beginnings and endings for your audio tracks, eliminate abrupt clicks, and create smooth transitions between clips. This guide covers the quickest methods to fade audio using built-in presets as well as how to customize your fade curves for complete control over your sound.

Method 1: Using the Standard Fade In and Fade Out Effects

This method applies a linear fade, which changes the volume at a constant, steady rate.

  1. Select the Audio: Click the Selection Tool (or press F1) and click-and-drag over the portion of the waveform where you want the volume transition to occur. For a fade in, highlight the start of the audio; for a fade out, highlight the end.
  2. Apply the Effect:
    • To gradually increase volume from silence, go to the top menu, select Effect, navigate to Fading, and click Fade In.
    • To gradually decrease volume to silence, go to the top menu, select Effect, navigate to Fading, and click Fade Out.
  3. Review the Result: Press the Spacebar to play back the selection and ensure the transition sounds natural.

Method 2: Using Studio Fade for a Musical Transition

If a linear fade sounds too abrupt, Audacity includes "Studio" fades, which apply curved, exponential volume adjustments that often sound more natural to the human ear.

  1. Highlight the section of audio you want to fade using the Selection Tool.
  2. Go to Effect > Fading.
  3. Choose either Studio Fade In or Studio Fade Out.
    • Studio Fade Out uses an S-curve that maintains musical presence longer before dropping off cleanly into silence.

Method 3: Using the Envelope Tool for Custom Fades

For complex or long gradual fades where you want precise control over specific volume levels over time, use the Envelope Tool.

  1. Select the Envelope Tool from the toolbar (or press F2). Two blue border lines will appear at the top and bottom of your audio track.
  2. Click on the border lines to create control points.
  3. Drag the control points inward to decrease volume or outward to restore full volume.
  4. Place multiple control points along the timeline to shape a completely custom, gradual volume ramp.
  5. Switch back to the Selection Tool (F1) once you are satisfied with the envelope shape.
