How to Create a Ping-Pong Delay in Audacity

Setting up a ping-pong stereo bouncing delay in Audacity adds dynamic spatial width to your audio by alternating echoes between the left and right stereo channels. While Audacity does not include a dedicated one-click ping-pong delay plugin by default, you can easily build one using track duplication, channel panning, and offset delay times. This guide explains step-by-step how to manually configure this effect using built-in Audacity tools.

Step 1: Prepare Your Source Audio

  1. Open your project in Audacity and select the audio track you want to process.
  2. If your track is in stereo, convert it to mono for the cleanest ping-pong effect by selecting Tracks > Mix > Mix Stereo Down to Mono.
  3. Keep this original track centered; it will serve as your "dry" (unaffected) signal.

Step 2: Create the Left and Right Delay Tracks

  1. Select the original track.
  2. Press Ctrl + D (or Cmd + D on Mac) twice to create two duplicate tracks.
  3. Rename the first duplicate to "Delay Left" and the second duplicate to "Delay Right" by clicking their track control panels.

Step 3: Pan the Duplicate Tracks

  1. Locate the Pan slider on the track control panel (on the left side of each track).
  2. Slide the Pan slider on "Delay Left" all the way to 100% Left.
  3. Slide the Pan slider on "Delay Right" all the way to 100% Right.
  4. Leave the original source track panned to the Center.

Step 4: Apply the Delay Effect

  1. Select the entire "Delay Left" track.
  2. Go to Effect > Delay and Reverb > Delay.
  3. Choose Regular as the delay type.
  4. Set your parameters:
    • Delay level per bounce (dB): -4 dB to -6 dB (so each bounce gradually fades).
    • Delay time (seconds): Choose your bounce tempo (for example, 0.40 seconds).
    • Number of bounces: Set between 3 and 6 depending on how long you want the tail.
  5. Click Apply.

Step 5: Offset the Right Channel

To create the alternating "ping-pong" bounce, the right channel must repeat at an offset interval half the duration of the left channel's delay time.

  1. Select the "Delay Right" track.
  2. Open the Delay effect (Effect > Delay and Reverb > Delay).
  3. Keep the Delay level and Number of bounces identical to Step 4.
  4. Keep the Delay time identical to the left track (e.g., 0.40 seconds), and click Apply.
  5. Use the Selection Tool to place your cursor at the very start of the "Delay Right" track.
  6. Go to Generate > Silence.
  7. Set the duration to exactly half of your delay time (e.g., 0.20 seconds if your delay time was 0.40 seconds) and click Generate. This shifts the right channel echoes directly between the left channel echoes.

Step 6: Balance the Mix

  1. Press the Spacebar to play the audio and listen to the bouncing effect.
  2. Lower the volume sliders on both the "Delay Left" and "Delay Right" track panels (typically between -6 dB and -12 dB) until the echoes sit comfortably behind the dry center track.
  3. When satisfied, export your project or go to Tracks > Mix > Mix and Render to combine the tracks into a finished stereo master.
