Who Oversees the Audacity Roadmap and Priorities?

Audacity’s feature roadmap and product development priorities are currently overseen by Muse Group, the multimedia company that acquired the open-source audio editor in 2021. While Audacity remains an open-source project licensed under the GPL, strategic decisions, user experience redesigns, and core feature implementations are guided by Muse Group’s internal product and engineering teams in conjunction with community feedback.

Muse Group’s Product Leadership

Following the acquisition in April 2021, Muse Group—known for properties like MuseScore, Ultimate Guitar, and StaffPad—established a dedicated development and product management unit for Audacity. Product leads and strategy directors at Muse Group, notably figures such as Martin Keary (Head of Product), took charge of shaping the long-term vision. This team is responsible for modernizing the software, introducing non-destructive editing, updating the user interface, and integrating cloud services such as audio.com for project sharing.

The Role of the Open-Source Community

Although Muse Group directs the high-level roadmap and allocates paid engineering resources to critical updates, the development process remains partially open to the wider community. Development takes place on GitHub, where:

  • Issue Tracking: Users and external contributors submit bug reports, feature requests, and usability feedback via GitHub Issues.
  • Pull Requests: External developers contribute code, patches, and feature improvements that the core team reviews and integrates.
  • Community Discussions: Developer forums and GitHub Discussions serve as sounding boards for proposed changes, allowing the user base to voice concerns and influence technical directions.

Core Priorities Shaping the Roadmap

The priorities established by Muse Group’s product managers currently focus on several key pillars:

  • Workflow Modernization: Replacing destructive editing workflows with modern, real-time effects and non-destructive clip handling.
  • Interface and Usability: Streamlining the classic UI to make Audacity more intuitive for podcasters, voice actors, and modern creators without sacrificing functionality for legacy users.
  • Stability and Code Modernization: Refactoring the legacy codebase to improve multi-track performance, cross-platform stability, and plugin compatibility (such as VST3 support).
