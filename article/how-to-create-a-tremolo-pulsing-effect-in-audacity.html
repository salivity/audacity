How to Create a Tremolo Pulsing Effect in Audacity

Creating a rhythmic volume pulsing effect in Audacity is a straightforward way to add movement, tension, and dynamic rhythm to flat audio tracks like synthesizer pads, guitars, or vocal layers. This guide explains how to apply and configure the built-in Tremolo effect to produce continuous volume pulses, synchronize the rate to your project's tempo, and choose between smooth modulation and choppy, gated effects.

Step 1: Select the Audio Track

  1. Open your project in Audacity and locate the track you want to modify.
  2. Use the Selection Tool (F1) to highlight the specific portion of the track where you want the volume pulse to occur, or double-click the track to select the entire waveform.

Step 2: Open the Tremolo Plugin

  1. Click on the Effect menu in the top toolbar.
  2. Navigate to Modulation (or scroll directly down the list in older Audacity versions) and select Tremolo.

Step 3: Configure the Pulse Settings

The Tremolo dialog box contains key controls that determine how the volume fluctuates:

  • Tremolo Amount (%): This sets the intensity or depth of the volume drop. A value around 40% to 60% adds subtle movement. Setting it between 80% and 100% cuts the volume almost entirely at the lowest point, producing an intense, dramatic pulse.
  • Tremolo Frequency (Hz): This dictates the speed of the pulse (cycles per second).
    • To match a specific tempo (BPM), calculate the frequency using:
      Quarter note pulse (Hz) = BPM / 60
      Eighth note pulse (Hz) = (BPM / 60) * 2
      Sixteenth note pulse (Hz) = (BPM / 60) * 4
    • For example, at 120 BPM, a quarter-note pulse requires a frequency of 2.0 Hz, while an eighth-note pulse requires 4.0 Hz.
  • Waveform Type: Choose the shape of the modulation:
    • Sine or Triangle: Produces a smooth, natural swelling and fading of volume.
    • Square: Instantly switches between full volume and silence, creating a rhythmic stutter or "chopper" effect.
  • Phase (Degrees): Set this slider to 0 to make both the left and right channels pulse simultaneously in volume. Setting this to 180 causes the channels to alternate, transforming the volume pulse into an auto-pan effect.

Step 4: Preview and Render

  1. Click the Preview button in the lower-left corner to audition the changes in real time.
  2. Fine-tune the Tremolo Amount and Tremolo Frequency sliders until the pulse fits your track's tempo and desired intensity.
  3. Click Apply to render the effect directly onto the selected waveform.
