How to Use the Auto Duck Effect in Audacity

The Auto Duck effect in Audacity automatically reduces the volume of background music whenever speech is detected, creating a balanced, professional mix for podcasts, videos, and voiceovers. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step walkthrough on properly arranging your audio tracks, configuring the Auto Duck settings, and applying the effect to achieve seamless audio transitions without manual volume adjustments.

1. Arrange Your Tracks Correctly

The Auto Duck effect relies on track placement to determine which audio controls the ducking process. Audacity requires the track being ducked to sit directly above the track triggering the ducking.

  • Top Track: Place your background music or ambient audio here.
  • Bottom Track: Place your speech or voiceover track directly underneath the music track.

If your tracks are not in this order, click the dropdown arrow next to the track name on the control panel (or the track title bar) and select Move Track Down or Move Track Up to position the music track above the voice track.

2. Select the Music Track

Auto Duck modifies only the selected audio. Click in the gray background area of the track control panel for the music track (the top track) to select it. Ensure the voice track is not selected, as you do not want to apply volume cuts to your speech.

3. Open the Auto Duck Effect

Navigate to the top menu and select Effect > Volume and Compression > Auto Duck (in older versions of Audacity, it is located directly under Effect > Auto Duck).

4. Adjust the Auto Duck Parameters

A dialog window will open displaying an interactive graph and several input fields:

  • Duck Amount: The volume reduction applied to the music. A setting between -12 dB and -24 dB usually allows speech to cut through clearly without silencing the music completely.
  • Fade Down Length: The time it takes for the music to lower once speech begins. A value between 0.2 and 0.5 seconds creates a smooth transition.
  • Fade Up Length: The time it takes for the music to return to normal volume after speech stops. Setting this between 0.5 and 1.0 seconds prevents abrupt volume spikes.
  • Duck Amount Threshold: The volume level the voice track must reach to trigger the ducking effect. If your music ducks during background noise or breathing, raise this threshold (make it closer to 0 dB).
  • Maximum Pause: How long the voice can pause before the music fades back up. A setting between 1.0 and 2.0 seconds prevents the music from jumping up and down during natural pauses between sentences.

5. Apply the Effect and Review

Click Preview to listen to the transitions, or click Apply (or OK) to process the track. Audacity will automatically generate volume envelopes that lower the music track whenever audio appears on the voice track below it. Play back your project to ensure the balance is natural, and press Ctrl+Z (Windows) or Cmd+Z (Mac) to undo and tweak settings if needed.
